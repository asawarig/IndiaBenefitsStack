Part 1 — Employee Benefits

Mandatory statutory insurance & benefits

Indian labour law mandates several contributory schemes. Thresholds are headcount and salary based. Non-compliance risks fines up to ₹1 lakh, criminal liability, and operational disruption.

Part 2 — Voluntary Employee Benefits

Recommended EB insurance for MNCs

Not legally mandatory, but market-standard for any MNC or GCC hiring professional talent in India. These benefits are a primary lever for retention — and candidates from IT, BFSI, and consulting sectors arrive with clear expectations about what a credible employer carries.

🏥
Group Health Insurance (GHI)
Floater or individual sum insured; employee + dependants
Recommended GCC standard

Any employer not relying solely on ESI. Virtually mandatory for employees above the ₹21,000/month ESI threshold — which covers most MNC white-collar hires.

7 employees to qualify for a standard group policy. Below this, insurers require individual retail policies (30–50% higher premium, with waiting periods).

Hospitalisation (inpatient), pre-existing conditions covered from day one, maternity, daycare procedures, cashless claim at insurer's network hospitals. Family floater commonly covers spouse + 2 children; parents can be added as top-up.

India median sum insured: ₹5,00,000. Global startups operating in India: ₹10,00,000. Top 10 percentile companies: ₹8,00,000 and above, regardless of headcount.

Pre-existing condition waiting period capped at 36 months (down from 48). Moratorium period reduced from 8 to 5 years. No upper age limit for enrolment in new policies.

Premium deductible for employer under Section 37(1) of the Income Tax Act. 18% GST applies on group policies. Not included in employee's perquisite income under Section 17(2).

🛡️
Group Personal Accident (GPA)
Accidental death, disability, income replacement
Recommended

Accidental death (typically 5–10× annual salary), permanent total disability, permanent partial disability. Often includes weekly compensation for temporary disablement and medical expense reimbursement.

GPA provides broader cover than mandatory Workmen's Compensation — covers non-occupational accidents and is not restricted to the workplace. MNCs typically hold both.

EDLI under EPF provides a maximum of ₹7 lakh death benefit. GPA fills the gap for senior employees and those with global pay structures where EDLI cover is structurally insufficient. At the 90th percentile, companies offer GPA of ₹50,00,000 or higher / 3–5× CTC.

💼
Group Term Life (GTL)
Death benefit; employer-paid life cover
Recommended GCC standard

Death benefit of 3–5× annual CTC. No medical underwriting for group of sufficient size. Often extended to include critical illness and accidental death rider.

Offered by 36% of 2–100 employee companies; 52% at 101–250; 75% at 251–750; 76% at 750+. Global startups in India: 30% adoption — lagging their GHI provision.

🩺
OPD / Wellness Benefits
Outpatient, mental health, preventive care
Recommended

Outpatient consultations, diagnostics, pharmacy reimbursements. Mental health support (EAP), telemedicine access, preventive health check-ups.

Only 9% of companies at 2–100 employees offer OPD; 14% at 101–250; 35% at 251–750; 60% at 750+. Global startups in India: 60% adoption — well above the India median of 16%.

Group health insurance primarily covers inpatient events — but Plum's telehealth data shows mental health (19% of consultations), dermatology (18%), and OB-GYN (10%) as the top day-to-day needs. These are overwhelmingly OPD interactions that a standard GHI policy does not address.

The parental cover decision: 40% of claims on ESCP (employee + spouse + children + parents) policies are for parents — and parents generate the highest average claim at ₹73,000. Part 3 — Business Insurance (Non-EB)

Commercial & liability insurance

Separate from the EB stack. These protect the MNC entity, its directors, its property, and its third-party obligations. Several are contractually required by clients, landlords, or parent-company risk frameworks before India operations begin.

⚖️
Directors & Officers Liability (D&O)
Personal liability protection for leadership
Recommended GCC standard

Defense costs, settlements, and judgments arising from claims by employees, shareholders, creditors, or regulators alleging wrongful acts — mismanagement, breach of duty, employment practices violations.

Under the Companies Act 2013, there is no bar on companies indemnifying directors and officers. Premium paid is not remuneration unless the director is proven guilty of negligence or misfeasance.

Fraud, wilful criminal acts, fines and penalties, anti-competitive behaviour, bodily injury to employees (covered under WC), professional errors by the entity (covered under PI).

India-incorporated subsidiary directors are personally exposed under the Companies Act from the CIN date. MNCs often extend a global D&O policy to India via a difference-in-conditions (DIC) local placement.

📋
Professional Indemnity (PI) / E&O
Errors, omissions, professional negligence
Sector-specific GCC standard

Claims arising from professional negligence, errors, or omissions in service delivery. Covers legal defense costs, settlements, and damages. Also typically covers intellectual property infringement and unintentional defamation.

CGL (below) explicitly excludes professional advice and service failures. PI fills that gap. IT services, consulting, BPO, financial services, legal, and engineering firms all require PI — CGL alone is insufficient.

Client contracts (especially Fortune 500 or public sector), RBI for regulated entities, SEBI for market intermediaries, and sectoral licensing requirements under IRDAI for insurance intermediaries.

🏢
Commercial General Liability (CGL)
Third-party bodily injury & property damage
Recommended

Third-party bodily injury on premises, property damage caused by the business, product liability (for manufacturers), completed operations liability.

Does NOT cover employee injuries (requires WC/EC) or professional advice errors (requires PI). CGL is always paired with WC cover for complete protection.

MNC clients routinely require a valid CGL certificate before vendor or supplier onboarding. Leased office space landlords may also require it as a condition of the lease.

IRDAI's target ICR: 70–90%. Avoid insurers with ICR above 95% (claims management risk) or below 50% (potentially underpaying claims).

🔐
Cyber Liability Insurance
Data breaches, ransomware, regulatory fines
Recommended GCC standard

Data breach response costs, forensic investigation, notification to affected parties, regulatory fines under DPDP Act 2023 / IT Act, business interruption from cyber incidents, ransomware payments (subject to conditions), third-party liability from data leaks.

India's DPDP Act 2023 creates significant financial exposure for data fiduciaries. Average cost of a data breach in India: ₹195 million (IBM, 2024). MNCs processing employee or customer data in India need standalone cyber cover — CGL does not cover cyber events.

43–46% of businesses in India faced at least one cyberattack in 2024. For GCCs processing global data flows, parent-company cyber programs may not extend to India operations; a local policy or endorsed global coverage is essential.

🏗️
Property / Fire & Allied Perils
Physical assets, office contents, equipment
Often contractual

Building structure (if owned), office contents, IT equipment, furniture, stock. Allied perils include earthquake, flood, storm, riot, and business interruption.

Not a standalone statutory requirement for most sectors, but lenders, landlords, and lease agreements typically make this a condition precedent. Standard Market Fire Policy (SMF) is the base form; "all risks" endorsements available.

🔍
Crime / Fidelity & Trade Credit
Internal fraud, employee dishonesty, credit risk
Sector-specific

Employee theft, fraud, forgery, and computer crime. Mandatory for BFSI-sector MNCs under RBI operational risk guidelines. Recommended for any entity with cash handling, treasury functions, or access to customer funds.

Default by B2B buyers on invoiced receivables. Relevant for MNCs with large Indian distribution networks or credit-extended channel partners.

Part 4 — Setup Stage

What to buy, and when

Insurance sequencing follows your entity and headcount milestones. Buying out of sequence creates coverage gaps and compliance risk.

Phase 1 — Entity registration (Day 1–90)

  • D&O Liability — directors are exposed from CIN date
  • Professional Indemnity — if client contracts already signed
  • CGL — required by landlord or client before operations commence
  • Cyber Liability — if any data processing begins
  • Property / Fire — required by lease agreement
  • Workmen's Compensation — any contract / blue-collar workers on-site

Phase 2 — First employees hired (7–19 employees)

  • Group Health Insurance — minimum 7 for group policy eligibility
  • Group Personal Accident — recommended from first hire; only 36% of small companies carry it, but GCCs are expected to
  • Group Term Life — standard with GHI for white-collar hiring
  • WC / EC mandatory for employees above ESI threshold
  • EDLI auto-enrolls via EPFO when EPF registration triggers

Phase 3 — Scale (20+ employees)

  • EPF mandatory — register with EPFO within 30 days of crossing 20
  • ESI mandatory — register with ESIC within 15 days of crossing 10
  • Gratuity fund or LIC gratuity scheme for provisioning
  • Crèche facility required at 50+ employees (Maternity Benefit Act)
  • OPD / wellness — 35% adoption at 251–750; competitive expectation from ~100 employees in GCC context
  • Review GHI sum insured: 90th percentile target is ₹10L at any headcount
⚠
EOR / PEO route: If setting up via an Employer of Record before entity incorporation, group insurance can be provided through the EOR's master policy, bypassing minimum group size thresholds. Confirm that the EOR's policy explicitly names the MNC and covers all in-scope employees.

Part 8 — Beyond Insurance

Group health policies are now required to include mental health parity — psychiatric ailments must be covered in hospitalisation on par with physical conditions. An EAP or therapy benefit remains a voluntary add-on, but the direction of travel is clear.

How to structure flex for an MNC

  • Base GHI plan: employer-funded, non-negotiable for all employees
  • Flex wallet: annual allowance (₹15,000–₹40,000 typical range) for employees to allocate
  • Parental cover: opt-in via flex wallet — employee-funded, employer-facilitated, group rates
  • Super top-up: employee can upgrade sum insured using flex budget
  • Wellness: gym, mental health, nutrition — chosen per employee
  • OPD add-on: optional, higher utilisation when employee-chosen
  • Annual review: reset flex allocations at renewal based on utilisation data

Benefits Plum doesn't place — but you still need a view on

An MNC's India EB programme goes beyond what any insurance broker places. These are the non-insurance benefits your global CHRO, your PEO, or your India HR lead should own. They're not in Plum's scope — but they belong in your India setup plan.

The non-insurance benefits layer

Each of these is either a regulatory requirement, a competitive necessity, or both. Global startups in India typically align these with their home-country standards — which sets the bar your local-hire candidates will increasingly know about.

Leave policy
Statutory: 12–15 earned leave days. Global MNC standard: 20–25 days + sick leave + public holidays. Unlimited leave is rare but growing in GCCs.
ESOPs / equity
Not a legal requirement, but effectively mandatory for senior GCC hires benchmarking against global comp. Indian ESOP taxation changed in 2021 — verify with a tax advisor.
Learning & development
₹20,000–₹60,000 annual L&D budget per employee is common in IT and GCC sector. Global startups in India often extend access to global L&D platforms (Coursera, LinkedIn Learning).
Hybrid / remote policy
Not regulated, but a significant offer-letter factor in Bangalore, Mumbai, and Hyderabad tech markets. Most GCCs offer 2–3 WFH days. Full-remote is still rare but increasing for senior specialist roles.
Food & transport
Meal allowance: ₹2,200/month is the tax-exempt limit. Transport allowance: ₹1,600/month tax-exempt. Both are standard in the Indian CTC structure — often included as allowances rather than cash reimbursements.
Internet & WFH stipend
Post-pandemic standard in tech companies. ₹1,500–₹3,000/month for internet reimbursement. One-time WFH setup allowance (₹10,000–₹25,000) offered by global startups entering India.
ℹ
The 1% reallocation test: If your total compensation budget is ₹20.2 crore, the difference between spending 1% on benefits (₹20L) and 2% (₹40L) is roughly 1 percentage point on annual salary increments — going from a 12% hike to an 11% hike. For that 1 percentage point, you move from a plan with ₹5L sum insured and no parental cover to one with ₹7–10L sum insured, parental cover, progressive maternity benefits, and primary healthcare access. 70% of employees would choose better benefits and a slightly lower salary over the reverse. Run this calculation before your next renewal conversation.
